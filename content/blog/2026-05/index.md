<span style="background-color: lightyellow;"><span style="font-size:22px">**(a) Microbiome and population health**</span></span>

{{< paper 
title="Microbiome signature of Parkinson’s disease in healthy and genetically at-risk individuals"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04318-5" 
journal="Nature Medicine, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Microbiota signatures could perhaps be used as part of earlier Parkinson disease diagnosis? In this metagenomics study, authors found 176 altered microbial species in PD cases, incl. 142 already altered in asymptomatic GBA1 carriers. Clever use of Cliff’s δ after DAA too (I think!).
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Lung microbiome predictors of epigenetic aging and potential associations with smoking and electronic cigarette use"
url="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13148-026-02126-9" 
journal="Clinical Epigenetics, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Interesting contribution to the microbiome-exposure-aging theme. Here, metatranscriptomics + lung DNAm clocks in (small number of) healthy individuals identifies live bacterial species predicting faster/slower lung aging (AUC 0.78-0.91).
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Paradigms for microbiome analysis in infectious and non-communicable diseases"
url="https://www.cell.com/trends/microbiology/fulltext/S0966-842X(25)00363-4" 
journal="Trends in Microbiology, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Interesting read on the challenges and paradigms for analysing the microbiome in infectious and NCDs.
{{< /paper >}}


{{< paper 
title="Habitual coffee intake shapes the gut microbiome and modifies host physiology and cognition"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71264-8" 
journal="Nature Communications, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Longitudinal shotgun metagenomics+fecal/urine metabolomics show habitual coffee intake shifts specific gut taxa and neuroactive metabolites, with abstinence partly reversing this. Decaf still rapidly changes the microbiome, interestingly (so not driven by caffeine?).
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="The female urogenital microbiome: Ecological insights, therapeutic strategies, and molecular mechanisms"
url="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S193131282600123X" 
journal="Cell Host & Microbe, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Very complete review, including good sections on probiotics/applications.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Gut microbiome predicts personalized responses to dietary fiber in prediabetes: a randomized, open-label trial"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66498-x" 
journal="Nature Communications, December 2025">}}
<b>Comment:</b> That’s cool. Randomized multicenter trial (n=802) shows baseline gut microbiome profiles can predict fiber response in prediabetes. Authors also identify and characterise 4 different responder clusters with improved glycemic control.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="The island biology of the host microbiome"
url="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0966842X26000405?dgcid=rss_sd_all" 
journal="Trends in Microbiology, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Really enjoyed this essay/review on the host microbiome through the lens of island biology. Great explanations, and a very good read throughout. The debate is open on how pervasive of an island the microbiome really is (is anything evolving as an island except islands under some very specific island-specific conditions? hmm.)
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Cayman enables large-scale analysis of gut microbiome carbohydrate-active enzyme repertoires"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-026-02318-2" 
journal="Nature Microbiology, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Finally something like this was developped. For all of us who have painfully tried to work with CAZymes and the clonky inflexible CAZyme encyclopedia online, this is extremely promising. This new too, Cayman, enables large-scale analysis of gut microbiome carbohydrate-active enzyme repertoires. Happy to hear feedback from those who tried it!
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Biogeography-associated emergence of enhanced oxygen tolerance in the abundant human gut commensal Segatella copri"
url="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1931312826001629" 
journal="Cell Host & Microbe, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Wow, very complete & cool study using ancient coprolite metagenomes + modern human sampling (+ in vivo/vitro exp) showing that _Segatella copri_ has evolved has evolved higher oxygen tolerance in industrialised populations via HGT of an oxyR stress response operon.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="The impact of artificial sweeteners on bacterial physiology and the microbiome"
url="https://www.cell.com/trends/microbiology/fulltext/S0966-842X%2826%2900010-7" 
journal="Trends in Microbiology, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Very complete summary of studies on artificial sweeteners vs. gut microbiota, showing impacts on growth, pathogen resistance, bacterial conjugation and natural transformation.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Studying low-dose antibiotic effects on the gut microbiome"
url="https://www.cell.com/trends/microbiology/fulltext/S0966-842X%2825%2900361-0" 
journal="Trends in Microbiology, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Something I always wanted to see more on (perhaps I have nostalgic flashbacks to my MSc project on _E. coli_ biofilm regulation by sub-MIC antibiotics back in… phew… the beginning of the century). Very nice review on why it can be interesting to look at the effect of low-doses of antibiotics on the gut microbiota.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Genetic predictors of GLP1 receptor agonist weight loss and side effects"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10330-z" 
journal="Nature, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Not technically about the microbiome but this was still interesting. Here’s a new GWAS in Nature to investigate why people respond differently to GLP-1 weight loss drugs. Authors find different alleles for GLP1R (targeted by semaglutide drugs) and GPIR (semaglutide and tirzepatide) linked to both, incl. to side effects of nausea/vomiting.
{{< /paper >}}

<span style="background-color: lightyellow;"><span style="font-size:22px">**(b) Microbial ecology and genomics, AMR, etc.**</span></span>

{{< paper 
title="Genomic and evolutionary factors influencing the prediction accuracy of optimal growth temperature in prokaryotes"
url="https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/msystems.00062-26" 
journal="mSystems, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> This study curated optimal growth temperature (OGT) and genomic data for ~2.8k bacterial species and built a new OGT prediction model. Interesting focus on bacteria vs. archaea too.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="IQ-TREE 3: Phylogenomic Inference Software using Complex Evolutionary Models"
url="https://academic.oup.com/mbe/article/43/5/msag117/8669857" 
journal="MBE, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> The latest version of this great phylogenetic tree inferrence method is out.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="First genome sequence and functional profiling of Psychrobacter SC65A.3 preserved in 5,000-year-old cave ice: insights into ancient resistome, antimicrobial potential, and enzymatic activities"
url="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1713017/full" 
journal="Frontiers in Microbiology, Feb 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> “Whole-genome analysis identified >100 AMR-associated genes, including clinically relevant determinants (e.g., _ampC, gyrA, gyrB, parC, parE, dfrA, rpoB, tetA, tetC_, and _mcr-1_), as well as multiple heavy-metal resistance and multidrug efflux genes” This excerpt wouldn’t be as cool to report if you didn’t know that it comes from a _Psychrobacter_ strain isolated from 5,000-year-old ice.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Interspecies interaction alters the trajectory of antibiotic resistance evolution by amplifying negative fitness epistasis"
url="https://academic.oup.com/ismej/article/20/1/wrag014/8459390" 
journal="The ISME Journal, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Interesting study suggesting that interspecies competition (_S. enterica_ + _E. coli_) results in “the selection of resistance mutations with higher levels of resistance and greater growth trade-offs”. There’s also a good spotlight of this in Trends in Microbiology here.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="The selfish ribosome"
url="https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003780" 
journal="Plos Biology, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> An interesting essay considering the ribosome as a selfish complex symbiont element, and the evolutionary consequences of this perspective.
{{< /paper >}}

<span style="background-color: lightyellow;"><span style="font-size:22px">**(c) Other general interests**</span></span>

{{< paper 
title="Single-cell analysis of the human immune system reveals sex-specific dynamics of immunosenescence"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-026-01099-x" 
journal="Nature Aging, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Here, authors ask whether male & female immune systems age differently at single-cell level across adulthood. Answer is yes: looking at PBMCs in 982 adults, they find immune aging is sex-biased with stronger female remodeling + some male-specific shifts too.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Prevalence of liver fibrosis in the general population (the LiverScreen project): a multinational European cohort study"
url="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2900354-5/fulltext" 
journal="The Lancet, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Study on 30,199 individuals from 9 EU countries finds that undiagnosed liver fibrosis is common in the general population and primarily driven by metabolic factors and alcohol consumption. Early detection is key to avoid cirrhosis progression and complications.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Emergency Department Opt-Out Testing for Hepatitis B: A Two-Year UK Multicentre Evaluation of Outcomes Across Seven Sites"
url="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/liv.70665" 
journal="Liver International, April 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Routine opt-out HepB testing in 7 UK emergency departments led to >2x assessments, found many undiagnosed or disengaged patients & linked most needing follow-up to specialist care. This works. .
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Eight vaccines linked to a lower risk of dementia"
url="https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/eight-vaccines-linked-lower-risk-dementia" 
journal="GAVI Article (not peer-reviewed), Apr 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Not a scientific article but a great report to read, summarizing that we are now starting to have extremely good data on the reduction of dementia risk associated with vaccination (shingles, flu, COVID, etc). The decrease in risk is phenomenal in these studies. Have a look, and get vaccinated people.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Multinational validation of the PREVENT and SCORE2 cardiovascular risk equations across 6.4 million individuals"
url="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04437-z" 
journal="Nature Medicine, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> Largest external validation of the 2 major CVD risk scores: PREVENT (AHA) & SCORE2 (used in Europe) across ~6.4M people worldwide. Both scores showed similar overall performance across regions + adding albuminuria as variable improved PREVENT discrimination.
{{< /paper >}}

{{< paper 
title="Once-weekly semaglutide versus placebo in patients with alcohol use disorder and comorbid obesity: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial"
url="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2900305-3/fulltext" 
journal="The Lancet, May 2026">}}
<b>Comment:</b> New trial in Lancet suggests that semaglutide may also help reduce heavy drinking in treatment-seeking patients with alcohol use disorder and obesity.
{{< /paper >}}
